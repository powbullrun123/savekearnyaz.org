The Crisis

What Happened & Why

The Globe Equity Decree of 1935 governs how much Gila River water Kearny receives each year. When Coolidge Dam runs low, Kearny's allotment drops first — because Kearny's water rights are among the most junior on the river.

💧
The Allotment Crisis
Kearny's normal maximum allotment is 610.38 acre-feet per year. On February 24, 2026, the Gila Water Commissioner issued Kearny a reallocated allotment of just 0.76 acre-feet for all of 2026 — less than 0.2% of normal.
610 → 0.76 ac/ft
🏜️
Coolidge Dam at Critical Low
Coolidge Dam has the capacity to store approximately 910,000 acre-feet of Gila River water. As of April 14, 2026, it held only 19,765 acre-feet — barely 2% of capacity. No snowpack melt means no replenishment in sight.
2% capacity
📜
The Globe Equity Decree of 1935
A federal court decree governing water rights along the Gila River. Kearny's rights are "junior" — meaning neighboring towns and tribal nations with older rights get water first. When the dam runs low, Kearny feels it first and hardest.
✅
What's Being Done Right Now
The Town is working with ASARCO, Resolution Copper, the Governor's Water Policy Office, Congressman Crane's office (EPA grant), Senator Rogers, County Attorney Miller, GRIC, and the Gila River Water Commissioner to find both short-term relief and a long-term solution.

Emergency Guidelines

What You Can & Cannot Do

The Town is under Water Conservation Level 5WE — the highest emergency level. These are not suggestions. They are emergency orders issued by Mayor Curtis Stacy under the authority of A.R.S. § 26-311.

🚫
No Lawn or Landscape Watering
Strictly prohibited. No exceptions for ornamental plants, grass, gardens, or shrubs using Kearny municipal water.
🚫
No Vehicle Washing
Strictly prohibited using Kearny water. Exception only for public health and safety vehicles.
🚫
No Pool Filling — Any Size
Strictly prohibited. Includes small kiddie pools, spas, and wading pools. Water can be hauled in from outside vendors.
🚫
No Driveway or Sidewalk Washing
Strictly prohibited. Dust control using lake water is permitted if you pump and haul it yourself.
✅
Sanitation & Life-Supporting Use
Bathing, cooking, drinking, laundry (reduced), dishes, and toilet use are permitted. Keep showers short. Re-wear clothing when possible.
✅
Grey Water Reuse — Encouraged
Capturing grey water from showers and sinks for outdoor plants is actively encouraged. This reduces municipal usage and helps the reclaim rate.
✅
Fix All Leaks Immediately
Check toilets — they're the most common source of hidden leaks. Town staff can assist with locating leaks. Call Town Hall if you need help.
✅
Haul Water for Non-Essentials
Landscaping water, pool water, and dust control water can be hauled in from outside vendors. Town Hall can point you to available vendors.
The Long-Term Solution

Project 88 — Kearny Basin Water Initiative

Project 88 is an independent water resource initiative focused on a single question: does Kearny already own the solution to its water crisis?

Our research of the ADWR public records has identified two deep-bore wells — Sites 23CCC (1,250 ft) and 23CAA (1,150 ft) — that show as "Data Gaps" in current records. At that depth, these wells punch through the alluvial layer the Globe Equity Decree governs and into fractured granite — a formation that produces cleaner, drought-resistant water from a completely separate legal classification called percolating groundwater.

The Decree's handcuffs have a geological floor. Project 88 is here to find it — and if the geology confirms what the data suggests, to help Kearny develop a water supply that no drought and no 1935 decree can touch.

Additionally, every gallon of treated effluent Kearny currently sends downstream could instead be injected back into these deep wells under an ADWR Constructed Underground Storage Facility permit — earning the Town Long-Term Storage Credits it owns and controls under ARS Title 45.

🔍 The Vault Wells

Two dormant wells at 1,150–1,250 ft depth with 18-inch casings — capable of 2,000+ GPM if rehabilitated. Infrastructure worth $500K–$1M to replicate already sitting inside city limits.

⚖️ Below the Decree

The Globe Equity Decree governs alluvial subflow — saturated Holocene sediment near the river surface. At 1,250 feet in fractured granite, these wells may access legally separate percolating groundwater outside the Decree's reach.

💧 The Effluent Strategy

Stop sending treated effluent downstream for free. Inject it back into the Vault wells and earn ADWR Long-Term Storage Credits — a water bank Kearny owns outright, recoverable on demand.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

How is Kearny's water allotment calculated?
The amount of water in Coolidge Dam (San Carlos Reservoir) determines Kearny's annual apportionment. With the dam at less than 2% capacity as of April 2026, the allotment was slashed to 0.76 acre-feet for the entire year.
Why are other towns like Hayden not affected the same way?
Kearny's water rights are "junior" — meaning they were established later than neighboring towns. Under the prior appropriation doctrine, senior rights get water first. Kearny feels the shortage before anyone else.
What happens if we run out of water?
You turn on your faucet and nothing comes out — for any purpose. That's the reality Mayor Stacy described in the April 8 emergency letter. The current trajectory without conservation had Kearny reaching zero on or around July 15.
What is the Town doing to find more water?
Active negotiations with ASARCO, Resolution Copper, GRIC, the Governor's Water Policy Office, Congressman Crane (EPA grant), Senator Rogers, and County Attorney Miller. Well 3 rehabilitation is underway targeting a July online date.
What is the reclaim percentage and why does it matter?
It measures how much water is returned to the wastewater treatment plant vs. how much is pumped out. The goal is 60-70%. Currently around 48-50%. Higher reclaim means less net draw on the allotment.
Can tribal nations take Kearny's deep well water?
Tribal water rights under the Globe Equity Decree apply to alluvial subflow — water in shallow river sediment. At 1,150–1,250 feet in granite, Project 88 believes these wells access percolating groundwater — a separate legal category the Decree does not govern. This requires formal hydrogeological confirmation.
